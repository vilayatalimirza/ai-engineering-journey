Lionel Andres Messi was born on June 24, 1987, in Rosario, Santa Fe Province, Argentina, to Jorge Messi, a department head at a pressed steel manufacturing plant, and Celia Cuccittini, who worked in a magnet manufacturing workshop. On his father's side, he is of Italian and Spanish descent, the great-grandson of immigrants from the Marche region of Italy, and on his mother's side, he has primarily Italian ancestry. Growing up in a tight-knit, football-loving family, he developed a passion for the sport from an early age, playing constantly with his older brothers, Rodrigo and Matias, and his cousins, Maximiliano and Emanuel Biancucchi, both of whom later became professional footballers. He also has a younger sister named Maria Sol. At the age of four, he joined the local football club Abanderado Grandoli where he was coached by his father. His maternal grandmother, Celia, accompanied him to training and matches. He was greatly affected by her death, shortly before his eleventh birthday, and since then, as a devout Catholic, he has celebrated his goals by looking up and pointing to the sky in tribute to his grandmother. He started playing for the Rosario-based football club Newell's Old Boys when he was seven years old, becoming a lifelong supporter of the club. During the six years he played for Newell's, he scored almost five hundred goals and was a member of The Machine of 87, a nearly unbeatable youth team named for the year of their birth. He regularly entertained crowds by performing ball tricks during half-time when the Newell's first team played home games. His goalscoring idol growing up was Brazilian striker Ronaldo, whom he called the best forward he had ever seen. His future as a professional player was threatened when, at the age of ten, he was diagnosed with a growth hormone deficiency. He began growth hormone therapy at age eleven, but his father's health insurance covered only two years of the therapy, which cost at least one thousand pesos per month. Newell's agreed to contribute but later reneged on their promise. He was scouted by Buenos Aires club River Plate, whose playmaker Pablo Aimar he idolized, and while they offered to pay for his medical treatment after he scored four goals at a trial, Newell's refused to release his player card, preventing the transfer. As his family had relatives in Catalonia, they sought to arrange a trial with Barcelona in September 2000, when he was thirteen. First-team director Carles Rexach immediately wanted to sign him, but the board of directors hesitated because it was highly unusual for European clubs to sign foreign players of such a young age. On December 14, an ultimatum was issued for Barcelona to prove their commitment, and Rexach, with no other paper at hand, wrote a contract on a paper napkin. In February 2001, his family relocated to Barcelona, where they moved into an apartment near the club's stadium, Camp Nou. During his first year in Spain, he rarely played with the Infantiles due to a transfer conflict with Newell's, and as a foreigner, he could only be fielded in friendlies and the Catalan league. Without football, he struggled to integrate into the team and was so quiet that some of his teammates initially believed he was mute. At home, he suffered from homesickness after his mother moved back to Rosario with his brothers and younger sister, while he stayed in Barcelona with his father. After a year at Barcelona's youth academy, La Masia, he was enrolled in the Royal Spanish Football Federation in February 2002. He could now play in all competitions, and he quickly befriended his teammates, including Cesc Fabregas and Gerard Pique. After completing his growth hormone therapy at age fourteen, he became an integral part of the Baby Dream Team, widely lauded as Barcelona's greatest-ever youth team. During his first full season, he was the top scorer with 36 goals in 30 games for the Cadetes A, who won an unprecedented treble of the league, the Spanish cup, and the Copa Catalunya. In the Copa Catalunya final, their victory over Espanyol came to be referred to as the final of the mask because, a week after suffering a broken cheekbone, he was allowed to play on the condition that he wear a protective plastic mask, which he eventually took off to score two goals in ten minutes. At the end of the season, he received an offer to join English club Arsenal but chose to remain in Barcelona. He continued to progress through the ranks at a rapid pace, debuting for four youth teams during the 2003 to 2004 season. After performing exceptionally well in international pre-season competitions with the Juvenil B, he played only one official match before being promoted to the Juvenil A, where he scored 18 goals in 11 league games. During an international break, he was called up to the first team, where his performance in a training session left veteran players astonished, with winger Ludovic Giuly recalling that he destroyed the team's starting center-backs and looked like an alien. At sixteen years old, he made his first-team debut in a friendly against Porto on November 16, 2003, and subsequently began training daily with the club's reserve team as well as weekly with the first team. Barcelona's star player Ronaldinho recognized his potential immediately, calling him his little brother and greatly easing his transition onto the first team. To gain further match experience, he joined Barcelona C, helping save them from relegation by scoring five goals in ten games. He signed his first professional contract on February 4, 2004, which contained an initial buyout clause of 30 million euros that automatically increased to 80 million euros when he debuted for Barcelona B a month later. He played five games with the B team that season and worked on increasing his muscle mass to compete with older, taller opponents. He began the 2004 to 2005 season as a guaranteed starter for the Barcelona B team but was soon promoted to the first team by manager Frank Rijkaard. He made his La Liga debut on October 16, 2004, at seventeen years old, and scored his first senior goal on May 1, 2005, becoming the youngest-ever scorer for the club at the time. On his eighteenth birthday, he signed his first contract as a senior team player, extending his stay through 2010 with a release clause of 150 million euros, which was quickly updated to double his salary. By the end of the 2005 to 2006 season, Barcelona had won La Liga and the UEFA Champions League. During the 2006 to 2007 season, he scored his first hat-trick in a Clasico against Real Madrid and scored two goals against Getafe and Espanyol that drew enduring comparisons to Diego Maradona's famous 1986 World Cup goals against England. Tactically, he had started his career in Spain as a left-winger, but Rijkaard moved him to the right wing so he could more easily cut through the defense into the middle of the pitch and curl shots on goal with his dominant left foot. The 2008 to 2009 season under new manager Pep Guardiola saw him take the number 10 shirt and become the tactical focal point of the team, often operating in a false nine role where he roamed the center and dropped deep into midfield to orchestrate plays. He scored 38 goals that season as Barcelona won the Copa del Rey, La Liga, and the Champions League, achieving the first treble in the history of Spanish football. During the 2009 to 2010 season, Barcelona won the Supercopa de Espana, the UEFA Super Cup, and the FIFA Club World Cup, becoming the first club to achieve the sextuple. He won his first Ballon d'Or, the FIFA World Player of the Year award, and his first European Golden Shoe, finishing the season with 47 goals in all competitions, equaling Ronaldo's club record. In the 2010 to 2011 season, Barcelona won a third consecutive La Liga title and another Champions League, while he won his second consecutive Ballon d'Or and became the club's all-time single-season top scorer with 53 goals. The 2011 to 2012 season was even more historic, as he won his third Ballon d'Or, became the top goalscorer in Barcelona's history at age 24 by overtaking Cesar Rodriguez, and finished with a record 50 goals in La Liga and 73 goals in all competitions, making him the single-season top scorer in the history of European club football. In 2012, he surpassed Gerd Muller's record for the most goals in a calendar year by scoring 91 times in all competitions, leading to an unprecedented fourth consecutive Ballon d'Or. His overall input into the team's attack had increased significantly, giving credence to the notion of Messidependencia, a perceived tactical and psychological dependence on their star player. To offset this load, Barcelona signed Brazilian forward Neymar from Santos before the 2013 to 2014 season. Under manager Luis Enrique in the 2014 to 2015 season, the team signed Uruguayan forward Luis Suarez, forming the devastating attacking trio known as MSN. Returning to the right wing position, he created a symbiotic relationship with his forward partners, culminating in a historic season. A hat-trick against Sevilla in November 2014 made him the all-time top scorer in La Liga, surpassing Telmo Zarra's 59-year record of 251 league goals. He led Barcelona to a historic second continental treble, earning his fifth Ballon d'Or while the MSN trio scored a total of 122 goals across all competitions, a Spanish record. The following year, the trio scored 131 goals, breaking their own record and winning domestic titles. He continued his individual dominance, winning his fourth European Golden Shoe in the 2016 to 2017 season with 54 goals overall. He signed a new deal in 2017 keeping him through 2021, and following the departure of Andres Iniesta in May 2018, he was named the team's new captain. In the 2018 to 2019 season, he won his sixth European Golden Shoe and clinched his sixth Ballon d'Or, but the club began to struggle systemically. Following a trophyless 2019 to 2020 season, he expressed to the club his desire to leave but ultimately decided to fulfill the final year of his contract. In his final season, he led the team to a Copa del Rey victory and claimed a record-breaking eighth Pichichi trophy, but severe financial constraints and structural obstacles posed by La Liga regulations prevented Barcelona from offering him a new contract despite his willingness to accept a fifty percent salary cut. On August 8, 2021, he held a tearful press conference at Camp Nou confirming his departure from the club where he had won a club-record 34 trophies, scored 672 goals, and cemented his legacy as one of the greatest footballers in history.
Two days after his emotional farewell remarks to Barcelona, on August 10, 2021, he joined French Ligue 1 club Paris Saint-Germain on a two-year contract with an option for an extra year. He chose the number 30 for his squad number, which was the number he wore when he made his senior debut for Barcelona. He scored his first goal for the club in a Champions League group stage win over his former manager Pep Guardiola's Manchester City team. He won his first Ligue 1 title, which was a record-tying tenth league title for Paris Saint-Germain, though he had an overall underwhelming domestic season, scoring six goals in the league. Near the start of the 2022 to 2023 season, he won his second trophy with the club in the Trophee des Champions. A goal against Nice saw him surpass Cristiano Ronaldo as the all-time highest goalscorer in European club football with 702 goals, and during that same match, he achieved his one thousandth career goal contribution at the club level. By the end of his second Ligue 1 season, he had scored sixteen goals and led the league with sixteen assists, helping Paris Saint-Germain clinch their eleventh Ligue 1 title. He was awarded the Ligue 1 Best Foreign Player of the Season before departing the club at the end of his contract. On July 15, 2023, Major League Soccer club Inter Miami announced his signing on a two-and-a-half-year contract. His unprecedented pay package set an American league record, with earnings from his salary, signing bonus, and an equity stake in the club reportedly surpassing fifty million dollars. His arrival in the United States caused a massive frenzy dubbed Messimania, with Inter Miami games selling out immediately and his jersey becoming the best-selling in the league. As the new team captain, he scored nine goals in his first six games and led Inter Miami to their first-ever trophy by defeating Nashville in the Leagues Cup final. On October 30, 2023, following his World Cup win with Argentina and his league success in France, he was awarded a record-extending eighth Ballon d'Or and was named Time magazine's Athlete of the Year, becoming the first footballer ever to win the award. During the 2024 season, he broke the record for the most assists in a single Major League Soccer game with five assists and scored two goals in a victory over the Columbus Crew to clinch the Supporters Shield award for Miami. He scored his first hat-trick for the club in a dominant regular-season finale, leading Miami to a league-record 74 points and earning the league's Most Valuable Player award. During the 2025 season, he became the fastest player in league history to reach forty league goals and won the Golden Boot as the top scorer with 29 goals and 19 assists. He signed a contract extension through 2028 and during the playoffs, he recorded an assist that pushed his career total to 405 across all competitions, surpassing Ferenc Puskas for the most career assists of all time. He led Miami to their first league championship by winning the 2025 MLS Cup, taking home the finals Most Valuable Player award and becoming the first player to win back-to-back league MVP honors. On March 18, 2026, he scored his 900th career goal during a CONCACAF Champions Cup match against Nashville, becoming only the second player in top-level men's football to reach the mark after Cristiano Ronaldo. As a dual citizen of Argentina and Spain, he was eligible to play for both national teams but chose his homeland. He led Argentina to victory in the 2005 FIFA World Youth Championship, winning the Golden Ball, and made his senior debut later that year at eighteen years old. At the 2006 FIFA World Cup, he became the youngest player to represent and score for Argentina at the tournament. He won an Olympic gold medal at the 2008 Summer Olympics in Beijing, and following the retirement of Juan Roman Riquelme in 2009, he inherited Argentina's iconic number 10 shirt. Despite being named captain in 2011, he endured a painful period of near-misses on the international stage. He led Argentina to the finals of the 2014 FIFA World Cup, where they lost to Germany, though he was awarded the Golden Ball as the tournament's best player. He then suffered agonizing consecutive defeats to Chile in the finals of the 2015 Copa America and the 2016 Copa America Centenario, during which he became Argentina's all-time leading goalscorer. The heartbreak of losing three major finals in three years caused him to announce his retirement from international football in 2016, but a nationwide campaign convinced him to return. Following a disappointing 2018 World Cup and a third-place finish at the 2019 Copa America, he finally led his team to the end of a 28-year trophy drought by defeating Brazil in the final of the 2021 Copa America, where he was named the best player of the tournament. He led Argentina to victory over Italy at the 2022 Finalissima before achieving the ultimate prize at the 2022 FIFA World Cup in Qatar. He scored twice in the final to defeat France, leading Argentina to their first World Cup victory in 36 years and becoming the first player to win the Golden Ball twice. In 2024, Argentina won the Copa America for a second consecutive time, and at the 2026 FIFA World Cup, he led his country to another final, winning the Silver Boot and Silver Ball, though they lost one to zero to Spain in extra time. On August 31, 2026, he announced his final retirement from international football as his nation's all-time leader in goals and appearances. His phenomenal success is built on a unique physical and technical profile. Due to his short stature, he has a lower center of gravity than taller players, giving him greater agility to change direction quickly and evade opposing tackles, earning him the Spanish nickname La Pulga Atomica, or The Atomic Flea. Despite being physically unimposing, he possesses significant upper-body strength to withstand physical challenges. His short, strong legs allow for rapid bursts of acceleration, while his quick feet enable him to retain absolute control of the ball when dribbling at high speeds. Predominantly left-footed, he is known for his positioning, quick reactions, and exceptional vision and range of passing. As his career advanced and his athleticism diminished with age, he began to dictate play in deeper areas of the pitch, developing into one of the best passers and playmakers in football history. By conserving his energy and running less off the ball, he improved his efficiency and avoided muscular injuries. Throughout his career, he has been continuously compared to Portuguese forward Cristiano Ronaldo, who is considered his primary career rival. While Ronaldo is praised for his exceptional speed, muscular athleticism, and goalscoring power, he is lauded for his magical dribbling, playmaking, and passing. The rivalry extends off the pitch into their contrasting personalities, with Ronaldo's self-confidence differing from his more unassuming humility. He has also been relentlessly compared to his compatriot Diego Maradona due to their similar short statures, left-footed playmaking styles, and shared nationality. Initially, he was held in lesser esteem than Maradona in Argentine society because of his early lack of international trophies and his reserved, introverted personality, which contrasted sharply with Maradona's fiery, extroverted nature. Some Argentines questioned his leadership and felt he was more Catalan than Argentine, but he made a conscious effort in 2019 to become more vocal and assertive, and his spectacular World Cup victory in 2022 finally cemented his status alongside Maradona in the hearts of his countrymen. Off the pitch, he is one of the most recognizable and wealthy figures in the world, appearing on Time's list of the most influential people in 2011, 2012, and 2023. His net worth surpassed one billion dollars in 2026, largely driven by record-breaking salaries and lucrative endorsements. He has held a long-term sponsorship with Adidas since 2006, receiving his own sub-brand of boots, and has served as a brand ambassador for companies like Pepsi, Gillette, Turkish Airlines, and Hard Rock Cafe. He also became a tourism ambassador for Saudi Arabia, which drew some human rights criticisms. His massive popularity is reflected in his social media presence, where he boasts over 500 million Instagram followers and holds the record for the most-liked post in history with his World Cup celebration photo. In 2024, he expanded his business portfolio by launching a sports drink brand named Mas+ and a global production company called 525 Rosario, and in 2026, he acquired full ownership of the Spanish football club UE Cornella. Since 2008, he has been in a relationship with Antonela Roccuzzo, the cousin of his childhood best friend, whom he married in 2017. They have three sons, and his professional affairs are tightly managed as a family business, with his father acting as his long-time agent until his passing in 2026, while his brothers and mother handle his scheduling, charitable foundation, and personal matters in his hometown of Rosario. Despite his immense wealth, his career faced legal trouble when he and his father were found guilty of tax fraud in Spain in 2016 regarding offshore companies used to evade taxes on sponsorship earnings, resulting in suspended 21-month prison sentences and heavy fines. Driven by his own childhood medical difficulties, he has dedicated significant resources to philanthropy, serving as a UNICEF Goodwill Ambassador and establishing the Leo Messi Foundation in 2007 to support healthcare, education, and sports access for vulnerable children. He has funded medical training, hospital developments in Argentina and Spain, youth academy facilities for his boyhood club Newell's Old Boys, and donated substantial sums to fight the COVID-19 pandemic and aid wildfire reconstruction in Madrid. In January 2025, in recognition of his extensive humanitarian contributions to children's healthcare and education globally, he was awarded the United States Presidential Medal of Freedom, cementing his legacy not only as one of the greatest athletes in history but also as a figure of profound global impact.
